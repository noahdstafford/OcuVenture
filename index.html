Independent bus travel for everyone

We are designing a way for visually impaired passengers to know when their bus arrives, identify it, and signal the driver to board.

Mission statement“Restore independence to people with visual impairments on every journey”

Our journey so far

Four weeks, from a broad commuter question to three screened concepts.

Week 2

Choosing a problem area

Surveys and interviews with stakeholders across Dublin led to our problem area: “Bus commuting is unreliable for those with visual or cognitive accessibility needs.”

Week 3

Needfinding

Outreach, interviews, a vision-simulation test and an affinity board produced our problem definition, prioritised needs and mission statement.

Week 4 · current

Concept generation and screening

6-3-5 brainstorming, benchmarking of existing products, then a Pugh chart screen. CAD and renders of the top three concepts are in progress.

The problem

Visually impaired passengers face barriers when using Dublin’s bus network, making independent travel difficult and uncertain.

Know when the bus is arriving

Arrival information that is reliable and real-time, so journeys can be planned with confidence.

Identify the correct bus

Knowing which bus is which, without relying on a display or on asking others.

Signal the driver to board

Communicating boarding intent without relying on a visible wave.

Benchmarking existing products

WeWALK

Pros: Works as a cane and phone, connects to the Moovit app.
Cons: Extremely expensive.

NaviLens

Pros: Hands-free, reads QR codes to scan for buses.
Cons: Doesn’t currently exist in Dublin.

Envision

Pros: Can read or describe everything you see.
Cons: Extremely expensive.

The Wayfinding Centre (TFI Independent Travel Support)

Pros: Free service that helps people across Dublin.
Cons: Only one site exists, making it less accessible.

Needs and requirements

Our affinity board grouped findings into themes of uncertainty, monitoring and signalling, and information availability.

Priority 1

Navigate independently

Find stops and routes and reach your destination.

Priority 2

Get clear real-time information

Know when the bus arrives and any changes.

Priority 3

Communicate discreetly

Signal that you need help, privately.

Requirement 1

Accuracy of arrival information

The system must give a reliable, real-time signal of bus arrival, so users can plan their journey with confidence.

Concept generation

Our three concepts

Concept A

Tactile info stand

Accessible smart bus stop with braille, raised lettering and tactile maps. In the Pugh chart it is a refreshable braille or pin display showing which buses are arriving and when.

Driver advance alert

Bus hailing and driver alert system. The driver’s console warns ahead of time that a visually impaired rider is waiting, with confirmation through an accessible interface.

Pugh chart

Each concept is scored against today’s setup (the datum): +1 better, 0 same, −1 worse. Change any weight (1–5) and the totals update. Our starting weights and scores are first guesses that the group is still refining.

Datum: audio push-button on real-time signs, phone apps, static braille stop signs, and asking the driver.
